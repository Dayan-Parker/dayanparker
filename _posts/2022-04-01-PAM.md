Both the truncated sinc function and raised cosine function have narrow time and frequency responses, making them ideal for PAM communication. Each of these pulse shapes was multiplied by cos(ω02πt) to shift its frequency response by ω0, where ω0 was the frequency band in which the data was sent. In this system, ω0 values of 10Hz, 20Hz, and 30Hz were used.

To simulate communications, three text signals were converted into binary data and used as the inputs to the system. Each signal was then upsampled to allow the pulse shapes to be convoluted with them will retaining the data. They were then convoluted with one of the frequency-shifted pulse shapes and then added to the other two signals, shown in Figure 5.

To recover the message sent originally from the up sampled, noisy data, a matched filter was used. To implement the matched filter, the noisy data was separated into each of the respective messages at 10Hz, 20Hz, and 30Hz by demodulating the signal with cos(ω022πt). The separate signals were then convoluted with a time reversed version of each frequency shifted pulse shape. To convert these signals back into binary data, the sign of their amplitude was 2 taken at every up-sampling period. A -1 was inserted if the sign was less than 0, and the +1 was inserted if the value was greater than zero. These binary signals were then graphed in comparison to the original binary message, shown in Figures 7, with a sigma value of 0.5.
